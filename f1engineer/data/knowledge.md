## Braking points and trail braking
Brake in a straight line at maximum pressure, then release progressively as you turn in (trail braking) so the front tyres keep load and the car rotates. Braking too early costs more time than braking slightly late: lose 10 m of braking and you lose roughly 0.05-0.1 s per corner. Use fixed markers (boards, kerb starts, shadows) and move the point 5 m at a time.

## Lock-ups
A lock-up happens when brake force exceeds front tyre grip. Fixes: less peak pressure, bias rearwards (lower front %), release brake earlier as you add steering, avoid braking on kerbs. Locking flat-spots tyres and makes the car run wide.

## Keyboard driving technique
Keyboard throttle, brake and steering are digital (0 or 100%). Tap throttle in short pulses to hold mid-corner speed instead of coasting, tap the steering instead of holding it, and use traction control medium and ABS while learning. Lower brake pressure (85-90%) makes full-brake taps less likely to lock. In Controls set steering saturation lower and linearity higher for smoother digital steering.

## Controller technique
Squeeze triggers instead of snapping them. Unwind the steering before applying full throttle. Small steering inputs at high speed; the car pivots on the brakes.

## Corner phases
Entry: braking and turn-in. Mid: minimum speed at the apex. Exit: throttle application and track-out. The exit onto a long straight matters most because the speed is carried down the whole straight. Sacrifice entry speed for a better exit before long straights (e.g. Silverstone Vale/Club, the Loop onto Wellington Straight, Stowe).

## Racing line
Outside-apex-outside maximises radius. Late apex for corners before straights. Missing the apex by 1 m forces a wide exit and later throttle.

## Understeer
Front tyres lose grip first; the car runs wide. Low speed fixes: softer front anti-roll bar, softer front suspension, more on-throttle diff opening. High speed: more front wing. Driving: brake earlier and in a straighter line, slower entry, rotate on trail brake.

## Oversteer
Rear loses grip; car spins. Exit oversteer: lower on-throttle differential, softer rear anti-roll bar, gentler throttle, higher gear. High-speed: more rear wing. Entry oversteer: more front brake bias, higher off-throttle diff.

## F1 2020 setup parameters
Front/rear wing 1-11 (downforce vs top speed). Differential on-throttle 50-100% (lower = more open = less wheelspin, better rotation on exit), off-throttle 50-100% (higher = more stable on entry). Camber: more negative = more cornering grip, more tyre heat. Toe: front toe-out = sharper turn-in; rear toe-in = stability. Suspension and anti-roll bars 1-11 (softer = more mechanical grip, slower response). Ride height lower = more downforce, risk bottoming. Brake pressure 50-100%; brake bias 50-70% front. Tyre pressure higher = less rolling resistance and more heat but less grip.

## Tyres
F1 2020 tyres work best with surface temps roughly 90-105 C (soft lower end). Wear above about 70% average falls off a cliff. Softs are fastest, hards last longest. Locking and wheelspin overheat tyres.

## ERS and fuel
ERS modes: none, medium, overtake (spend), hotlap. Use overtake on straights to attack or defend, harvest in the slow sections. Fuel mix lean saves fuel; lift-and-coast before braking zones saves fuel with little lap time cost.

## Race strategy
Pit when tyres approach the cliff or when the pit loss is reduced (safety car roughly halves it, VSC reduces it by around 40%). Undercut: pit first and use fresh-tyre pace when the gap ahead is smaller than the fresh-tyre advantage over one or two laps. Overcut works when tyres are not degrading and track is clear.

## Silverstone
High-speed track. Key corners: Maggotts-Becketts-Chapel (rhythm, minimal braking, commit), Copse (fast right, flat or a lift), the Loop (slowest corner, get the exit onto Wellington Straight), Village, Brooklands-Luffield (long double apex, patience on throttle), Stowe (heavy braking after Hangar Straight), Vale-Club (heavy braking, exit onto the pit straight is critical). Medium-high downforce setup.

## Which game: F1 2020
2019-spec cars (13-inch tyres, high downforce, DRS, MGU-K + MGU-H hybrid). Setup wings 1-11. My Team career mode debuted. Telemetry format 2020: everything the engineer needs comes in the standard packets, including tyre wear in Car Status. Time Trial shows personal-best and rival ghosts.

## Which game: F1 2021
Same generation of car as 2020 with refined handling, Braking Point story mode, two-player career. Telemetry format 2021 (also offers 2020): lap times in milliseconds, tyre wear and damage moved to a separate Car Damage packet, controller buttons sent as events. Tyre surface window similar to 2020, roughly 90-105 C.

## Which game: F1 22
2022 ground-effect regulations: 18-inch wheels, heavier and lower cars with much less dirty air, so following and overtaking are easier but the cars are lazier in slow corners and sensitive to ride height (bottoming over kerbs at low ride height). Setup wing values run 0-50 (finer steps than 2020). Adaptive AI, F1 Life, supercars. Expect more understeer in slow corners: patience on throttle, use the brakes to rotate. Telemetry format 2022.

## Which game: F1 23
Reworked handling with more front-end grip and better controller feel, red flags, 35 percent race distance option, Precision Drive for controllers, Braking Point 2. Wheel slip and suspension data moved to a Motion Ex packet. Telemetry format 2023.

## Which game: F1 24
New suspension and tyre model with more mechanical grip and more realistic kerb behaviour; ERS deployment reworked; sprint shootout sessions; Driver Career with recognition. Cars feel more planted mid-corner but punish aggressive kerb use and wheelspin out of slow corners. Telemetry format 2024.

## Which game: F1 25
Laser-scanned tracks (for example Bahrain, Suzuka, Miami, Imola, Melbourne), reverse layouts (Silverstone, Austria and Zandvoort reversed), My Team 2.0 with a team-owner role, Braking Point 3. Handling refines F1 24. Telemetry format 2025.

## Which game: 2026 season (F1 25 update)
EA delivered the 2026 season as an expansion for F1 25 rather than a separate F1 26 game. Real 2026 regulations: smaller, lighter cars on narrower tyres; power split about 50/50 between the combustion engine and a stronger MGU-K (no MGU-H); sustainable fuel; active aerodynamics with a low-drag straight-line mode instead of DRS, plus a manual energy-boost overtake mode. Driving implications: energy management matters far more (harvest under braking and lift-and-coast, deploy on exits and straights), less downforce means earlier braking and more mechanical-grip setups. How closely the game models these is not confirmed; the engineer reads its telemetry like F1 25's.

## Setup differences between games
F1 2020 and 2021: wings 1-11, suspension and anti-roll bars 1-11, differential 50-100 percent. F1 22 onward: wings 0-50 and finer suspension steps; ground-effect cars want ride height as low as possible without bottoming (raise it at bumpy tracks or if the car bottoms over kerbs). The direction of every change is the same in all games: softer front = more front grip in slow corners, more wing = more grip and less top speed, lower on-throttle diff = less wheelspin.

## Tyre temperatures by game
Aim for surface temperatures roughly 85-105 C on slicks in every game (softs at the lower end); inner (carcass) temperatures roughly 90-110 C. Overheating rears (above about 110 C surface) cause exit oversteer: smoother throttle, less wheelspin, lower on-throttle diff. Cold tyres after a pit stop or safety car need two or three corners of gentle braking.

## Melbourne (Albert Park)
Semi-street circuit, fast since the 2022 layout change. Key: the fast left-right chicane at turns 9-10, the stop at turn 3 after the start, and the exit of turn 13. Medium downforce.

## Sakhir (Bahrain)
Abrasive surface, high rear tyre wear and big traction zones. Key: turn 1 heavy braking, turn 4 exit, turns 9-10 (downhill braking while turning, easy to lock the inside front), turn 11 exit onto the back straight. Save the rears on exits.

## Jeddah
Very fast street circuit with blind high-speed sweeps and walls close by. Key: rhythm through the esses (turns 4-12) and the final hairpin exit onto the main straight. Low-medium downforce.

## Shanghai
Long turns 1-2 snail (patience, late throttle), turn 6 hairpin, the very long back straight into the turn 14 hairpin (big braking and overtaking). Front-left tyre wear is high.

## Suzuka
Figure-eight and technical. The S curves (turns 2-7) are all about rhythm and the line, then Degner 1 and 2, the hairpin, Spoon (exit onto the long straight), 130R (flat or nearly), Casio chicane. High downforce.

## Miami
Street-style circuit around the stadium. Slow chicane at turns 14-15, long back straight into turn 17, flowing high-speed section turns 4-8. Low grip off-line.

## Imola
Old-school, narrow, kerbs everywhere. Tamburello and Villeneuve chicanes, Tosa hairpin, Piratella, Acque Minerali, Variante Alta. Overtaking is hard, so qualifying matters.

## Monaco
Slowest, narrowest track: maximum downforce, softest suspension. Key: Sainte Devote, Massenet and Casino, Mirabeau, the hairpin (tightest corner of the year), Portier exit into the tunnel, Nouvelle Chicane, Swimming Pool, Rascasse. No overtaking: qualifying is everything and smooth inputs beat bravery.

## Catalunya (Barcelona)
Aero test track: long turn 3, fast turn 9, slow final sector. High front-left tyre wear. Medium-high downforce.

## Montreal
Stop-start: big braking zones into hairpins and chicanes, walls (Wall of Champions at the final chicane), kerb hopping. Braking stability and traction matter most. Low-medium downforce.

## Austria (Red Bull Ring)
Short lap with three big braking zones (turns 1, 3, 4) and fast final corners (turns 9-10). Track limits are strict at turns 1, 9 and 10. Medium downforce.

## Hungaroring
Twisty, slow and hot: high downforce. Overtaking mostly into turn 1. Rhythm through turns 4-11 and a good exit of the last corner.

## Spa
Longest lap, often mixed weather. Eau Rouge and Raidillon (commit), Kemmel straight, Les Combes, Pouhon (fast double-apex left), Blanchimont, Bus Stop chicane. Low-medium downforce because of the long straights.

## Zandvoort
Narrow and undulating with banked turns 3 (Hugenholtz) and 14 (Arie Luyendyk). Use the banking: carry speed and take a tighter line. High downforce.

## Monza
Temple of speed: lowest downforce. Heavy braking into the first chicane (Rettifilo), Roggia, the Lesmos, Ascari, Parabolica exit onto the main straight. Brake stability and slipstream matter most.

## Baku
Street circuit with a 2 km flat-out run and the narrow castle section. Very low downforce for the straight, but brakes and traction into 90-degree corners. Walls punish mistakes.

## Singapore
Night street race, hot and humid, bumpy, many 90-degree corners: maximum downforce, traction and brake management.

## Texas (COTA)
Uphill turn 1 (late apex), esses turns 3-9 (rhythm like Becketts), long back straight into turn 12, stadium section. Bumpy. Medium downforce.

## Mexico
High altitude: thin air means less downforce and less cooling even with maximum wing. Long straight into turn 1, stadium section. Brakes and engine run hot.

## Brazil (Interlagos)
Anticlockwise and short with elevation. Senna S at turns 1-2, Descida do Lago, and the Juncao exit (leads up the long climb to the line). Weather changes often.

## Las Vegas
Night street circuit, cold track (tyres hard to warm), a very long Strip straight, low downforce. Warm the tyres on out-laps and after safety cars.

## Losail (Qatar)
Fast, flowing corners with high lateral load and heavy tyre stress; kerbs are aggressive. Medium-high downforce.

## Abu Dhabi
Long straights since the 2021 layout change, hotel section at the end. Turn 5 hairpin and turn 9 braking zones for overtaking. Traction out of slow corners.

## Paul Ricard
Long Mistral straight with the chicane, fast Signes, the long double-right Beausset.

## Portimao
Rollercoaster elevation and blind crests. Commit over the crests and nail the exit of the final long right onto the straight.

## Sochi
Long sweeping turn 3, many 90-degree corners, smooth surface with low wear. Traction on exits.

## Hockenheim
Hairpin at turn 6 after the long straight, stadium section at the end. Medium downforce.

## Hanoi
Street circuit with a 1.5 km straight and a tight twisty section (in F1 2020 only).
